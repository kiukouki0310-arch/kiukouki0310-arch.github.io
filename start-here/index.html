Start here: eating in Japan with dietary needs

Japan is a wonderful place to eat, but wheat hides in soy sauce, fish stock hides in "vegetable" soup, and pork and alcohol sit in sauces and seasonings. This page is the quickest route through our guides.

The 3 things to do before you go

  1. Learn where the hidden ingredients are — soy sauce (wheat), dashi (fish), mirin and sake (alcohol), lard and meat extracts.
  2. Carry your request in written Japanese — staff understand a card far better than spoken English.
  3. Arrange meals in advance — hotels, ryokan and restaurants can often adapt with notice.
